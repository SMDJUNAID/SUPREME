Cold Chain and Packaging Best Practices for Exporting Injectable Medicines

Injectables demand tighter temperature control than most pharmaceutical exports. Here's what buyers should verify to make sure potency and safety are preserved in transit.

Published: 16 August 2026 • Category: Logistics & Quality

Injectable medicines are among the most sensitive products in international pharmaceutical trade. A break in temperature control at any point — warehouse, airport tarmac, or customs holding area — can compromise an entire shipment. Buyers who understand the cold chain can ask sharper questions of their suppliers and protect their investment.

Why temperature control is non-negotiable

Most injectables are formulated to remain stable within a defined temperature range, commonly 2–8°C for cold chain products or below 25–30°C for controlled room temperature items. Exposure outside this range, even briefly, can reduce potency or compromise sterility.

Packaging elements that protect the product

  • Insulated shipping containers rated for the required temperature range.
  • Validated cold packs or phase-change material suited to the transit duration.
  • Temperature data loggers included in the shipment for verification on arrival.
  • Tamper-evident seals and shock-resistant secondary packaging.

Verifying the supplier's cold chain process

Ask suppliers how they validate packaging for the specific transit time and climate to your destination. A supplier who tests packaging performance for hot climates like West or East Africa is better prepared than one using generic global packaging specs.

Coordinating with logistics partners

Cold chain integrity depends on more than packaging. Confirm that freight forwarders and customs clearing agents understand the shipment is temperature-sensitive, and that transit and clearance times are minimised, particularly during layovers or customs inspection.

Receiving and storage on arrival

The cold chain doesn't end at the airport. Buyers should have pre-cooled storage ready before the shipment lands, and should inspect the data logger and packaging condition immediately on receipt, before signing off on delivery.
